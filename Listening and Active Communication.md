# Listening and Active Communication
**Question 1: What are the steps/strategies to do Active Listening?**

   Active listening means giving full attention to the speaker and making sure you understand their message clearly. It requires respect, patience and involvement from the listener. Below are some strategies that strengthen active listening:

* Pay full attention and remove distractions.

* Show you are listening using nods or short verbal cues.

* Avoid interrupting and wait for the speaker to finish.

* Paraphrase what they said in your own words.

* Ask questions to clarify anything unclear.

* Notice body language, tone, and emotions.

**Question 2: According to Fisher’s model, what are the key points of Reflective Listening?** 

Reflective listening focuses on understanding both what the speaker says and how they feel. Fisher’s model encourages listeners to create a safe space where speakers feel heard and valued. It is more about understanding than responding.

* Restate or paraphrase the speaker’s message.

* Check if your interpretation is correct.

* Pay attention to the feelings behind the words.


**Question 3: What are the obstacles in your listening process?**

I face several challenges that affect how well I listen. These obstacles reduce my ability to understand others and make communication less effective.

* Getting distracted by my phone or surroundings.

* Thinking about my reply while the person is still talking.

* Interrupting due to impatience or excitement.


**Question 4: What can you do to improve your listening?**

To improve my listening, I need to make conscious efforts to control my distractions and stay mentally present. Small changes in behaviour can significantly increase the quality of communication.

* Put my phone away and minimise distractions.

* Maintain eye contact to stay engaged.

* Wait patiently without interrupting.


**Question 5: When do you switch to Passive communication style in your day-to-day life?**

I usually shift to passive communication when I feel uncomfortable expressing my thoughts. This often happens when I don’t want to upset someone or when I feel less confident.

* When I want to avoid conflicts.

* When the other person is more dominant.

* When I feel my opinion will be ignored.


**Question 6: When do you switch into Aggressive communication style in your day-to-day life?**

Aggressive communication appears when my emotions take over. It usually happens when I am frustrated or feel like I am not being respected or heard.

* When I feel stressed or overwhelmed.

* When someone keeps interrupting me.

* When the situation feels urgent.

**Question 7: When do you switch into Passive-Aggressive communication style in your day-to-day life?**

I usually switch to passive-aggressive behaviour when I want to express dissatisfaction but don’t feel comfortable doing it openly. It becomes a way of releasing frustration indirectly.

* When I use sarcasm instead of explaining my feelings.

* When I stay silent but remain upset.

* When I avoid the person instead of addressing the issue.

**Question 8: How can you make your communication assertive?**

Assertive communication is a balance between being respectful and being confident. It means expressing my thoughts clearly while also considering the other person’s perspective.

* Use “I” statements to express my feelings.

* Keep a calm tone and maintain eye contact.

* Set clear boundaries (say “no” politely).

* Express emotions directly, not through sarcasm.
