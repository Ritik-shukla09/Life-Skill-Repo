## 2. Tiny Habits by BJ Fogg – Core Message
### Question 2
**How can you use B = MAP to make new habits easier? What are M, A, and P?**

**Answer:**  
The formula **B = MAP** means Behavior happens when **Motivation (M)**, **Ability (A)**, and a **Prompt (P)** come together.  
To make habits easier:  
- Reduce the required **Motivation** by making the habit tiny.  
- Increase **Ability** by making the habit simpler.  
- Use a clear **Prompt** (an existing routine) to remind you at the right moment.

---

### Question 3
**Why is it important to "Shine" or celebrate after each successful completion of a habit?**

**Answer:**  
Celebration creates a positive emotion called *“shine”*, which tells the brain: *“This behavior feels good—repeat it.”*  
This emotional reward wires the habit faster and makes the behavior more automatic over time.

## 4. Book Summary of Atomic Habits
### Question 5
**What is the book's perspective about Identity?**

**Answer:**  
Atomic Habits teaches that true change starts with *identity*, not goals. Instead of focusing on what you want to achieve, focus on *who you want to become*. Your habits should reinforce your identity—for example: “I am a healthy person” instead of “I want to lose weight.”

---

### Question 6
**Write about the book's perspective on how to make a habit easier to do.**

**Answer:**  
Make the habit easy by reducing friction—prepare your environment, break the habit into smaller steps, and use the “2-minute rule,” where the habit is simplified so much that it takes less than 2 minutes to start.

---

### Question 7
**Write about the book's perspective on how to make a habit harder to do.**

**Answer:**  
Increase friction by adding steps or obstacles between you and the unwanted habit. Hide the cues, remove easy access, delay the behavior, or make it less convenient—these changes weaken the habit loop.

## 5. Reflection

### Question 8
**Pick one habit that you would like to do more of as a professional software developer. What steps can you take to make the cue obvious or the habit more attractive or easy and/or the response satisfying?**

**Answer:**  
**Habit:** Improving my skills by reading technical documentation or articles for 10 minutes each day.

**Steps:**  
- Set a fixed time (e.g., right after starting my workday) and keep the documentation tab bookmarked and highlighted (cue obvious).  
- Choose topics directly related to my current projects—making the learning feel useful and immediately applicable (make it attractive).  
- Start with very small sections like one paragraph or one concept per day so that it requires minimal effort to begin (make it easy).  
- Maintain a “learning log” or GitHub Gist where I record what I learned, creating visible progress that feels rewarding (make it satisfying).

### Question 9
**Pick one habit that you would like to eliminate or do less of. What steps can you take to make the cue invisible, the process unattractive or hard, or the response unsatisfying?**

**Answer:**  
**Habit:** Scrolling social media late at night.  
**Steps:**  
- Keep the phone outside the bedroom (make cue invisible).  
- Turn phone to grayscale (make it unattractive).  
- Log out of apps daily (make it harder).  
- Set a penalty rule like losing screen time the next day (make it unsatisfying).
